01 — About

A mathematician who moved to the markets

For most of my career I taught mathematics — partial differential equations, linear algebra, graph theory and operations research — to engineering students across Gujarat. A Ph.D. on parabolic PDEs, an M.Phil. in financial mathematics, six peer-reviewed papers, two design patents, and a Best Teacher award along the way.

The financial mathematics was always the thread. Since 2026 I have worked as an independent stock market analyst, focused on the part of the Indian market where the documentation is richest and the reading is thinnest: Initial Public Offerings.

Almost every IPO conversation starts and ends with "should I apply?" — a question about the next five days. I am interested in the one that decides the next five years: What am I actually buying, and what am I being asked to pay for it? The answer is in the prospectus, in the cash flow statement nobody opens.

What I'm working on now

  • Pre-IPO reads. A full RHP/DRHP breakdown before bidding opens — business, use of proceeds, numbers and cash, promoters, risks, and price.
  • Post-listing follow-ups. Seven checks every quarter after listing day, when most coverage has already stopped.
  • Sector & macro notes. Seasonality studies, liquidity, rates and what they mean for Indian equities.
  • Tools. A free 21-question IPO test and calculator that runs entirely in your browser.

The OWNERS framework

Every prospectus I read goes through the same six sections, in the same order — business first, price last.

  1. O

    Operations

    What the company actually does, and how it makes money.

  2. W

    Why the money

    Fresh issue or offer for sale — how much reaches the company.

  3. N

    Numbers & cash

    Whether reported profit ever became cash in the bank.

  4. E

    Executives & promoters

    Who runs it, who owns it, and who is selling.

  5. R

    Risks & rivals

    The disclosed risks and the listed peer set, on like-for-like terms.

  6. S

    Sticker price

    What the band implies — P/E, P/B, market cap — and what it requires.
